Recording brief

Four videos, about 40 seconds total

Short, crisp, immediately legible. Every one of these should be understandable with the sound off and no caption read.

Global settings. Record at 2× the display size and scale down — text on the card is small. Hide the cursor except where noted. Kill the theme toggle in frame if you can. Use the dark theme throughout, it matches all three pages. Export as MP4 and WebM; drop them in media/ and swap the placeholder .media-empty blocks for a <video autoplay muted loop playsinline>.

Loop-friendly means: start and end on the same frame. For 01 that's the resting card; for 03 it's the deck centred on the same card you started on.

02

The ship sequence, in full

12–14s16:10cursor on the drag onlyappears in all 3
  1. Start on the rest beat — "One of one." visible above the card.
  2. Click the forward arrow. Show this click — it's the moment you're arming it.
  3. Hide the cursor for the whole sequence. Let it run untouched to the envelope at rest.
  4. Cursor returns, grabs the envelope, drags down into the slot. Deliberately release early, above halfway-ish — let the magnetic assist finish it. That's the point.

Zoom: punch to ~130% on the first fold, hold through the folds, pull back to 100% when the envelope appears so the slot and envelope are both in frame for the drag.

Watch for: this is 5s of animation plus a drag. If it runs past 14s, cut the rest beat down to half a second rather than speeding anything up.

03

The deck and the sliders

10–12s16:10cursor visibleappears in all 3
  1. Drag through four or five cards — one continuous gesture, let it settle with the spring, don't click through.
  2. Land on a card. Pause half a second.
  3. Drag the spacing slider full-left to full-right, slowly. The pattern should visibly breathe.
  4. Drag frequency across.
  5. Now drag the deck one card sideways — this is the shot: the sliders spring to the new card's values on their own.

Zoom: stay at 100% for the deck drag. Punch to ~140% on the slider row for steps 3–4 with the card still partly in frame. Pull back for step 5 so both the card change and the slider retarget are visible in one frame.

Watch for: step 5 is the micro-magic moment and it's easy to miss. Consider a click-ring emphasis on the slider thumbs as they move by themselves.

Before you publish

Five things to fix from the current live page

number

"54 million" checks out — show the working. cardConfigToParams serializes both sliders with .toFixed(3), so each carries 1001 distinct values. 9 × 3 × 2 × 1001 × 1001 = 54,108,054 distinct shareable states. That's not a marketing number, it's the exact size of the space your share URL can encode.

All three drafts now keep the number and add a diagram deriving it, plus one line of framing: the number isn't the achievement — making all 54 million worth having is. Stating it that way pre-empts the reviewer who does the arithmetic, and turns a volume claim into a constraint claim.

wording

"Credit card." The live page says credit card throughout. The product is a challenger-bank card that arrives in the post — there's no credit product anywhere in it. All three drafts just say "card".

contradiction

Reduced motion is listed under "what's next" — but it ships. The wrap collapses to crossfades, the deck flattens, the drag becomes a button. Either the writeup undersells shipped work or the README oversells it. Confirm which, then fix one of them. All three drafts currently assume it ships, in the "cut, deliberately" panel's counterpart.

missing

The URL personalization isn't in the live writeup at all, even though your demo link uses it. It's the cheapest magic moment in the build and possibly the highest-leverage: you can send a reviewer a link with their own name on the card. All three drafts give it a section and a video.

decide

The engraving note field. Built, plumbed, URL param and all — then unmounted from the panel. Right now it's invisible in both the app and the writeup. All three drafts list it under "cut, deliberately", which is the honest framing. If you'd rather it not appear at all, delete that line — but then also don't mention "your words" anywhere.
